# Research notes

## Market and cost

Lane Z studies **equity perpetuals**. NVDA and TSLA refer to Trade[XYZ] contracts on Hyperliquid with price exposure to those stocks. HIP-3 deployers define the contracts and maintain the oracle and risk parameters. The displayed mark can affect margin and liquidation, the oracle is a funding reference, and trades occur against the perp order book. These three prices answer different questions. [Hyperliquid HIP-3 specification](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hyperliquid-improvement-proposals-hips/hip-3-builder-deployed-perpetuals), [Trade[XYZ] oracle mechanics](https://docs.trade.xyz/perpetuals/mechanics/oracle-price/).

Trade[XYZ] switches between external pricing, when inputs are available, and an internal mechanism driven by the impact bid and ask. Scheduled U.S. equity closures and contract-specific rules matter when defining a dataset. The August 13 changelog moved the Sunday end of the internal session five minutes later. Lane Z studies the much narrower 09:35–15:55 Eastern windows on weekday sessions and on weekends or New Year's Day. Scheduled reopenings, pre-market and unscheduled halts call for their own event study. [Trade[XYZ] session changelog](https://docs.trade.xyz/perpetuals/changelog/equities-internal-session-hours/), [Trade[XYZ] holiday schedule](https://docs.trade.xyz/perpetuals/specifications-and-schedules/holiday-closures/), [NYSE holiday calendar](https://www.nyse.com/trade/hours-calendars).

The documented Tier 0 Trade[XYZ] growth rates are **0.9 bps taker and 0.3 bps maker per side**. Standard rates are 9 and 3 bps respectively. On the public `/info` `meta` request for dex `xyz`, both target contracts reported growth mode enabled, deployer fee scale 1.0 and the last fee scale change timestamp on November 23, 2025. The replay uses the growth fee and stresses the standard rate. Actual fees depend on account tier and changes to market settings. Hourly funding can matter if the exit is delayed. [Trade[XYZ] fee schedule](https://docs.trade.xyz/perpetuals/mechanics/fees/), [Hyperliquid fees and formula](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/fees), [Hyperliquid API](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/info-endpoint), [Hyperliquid funding](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/trading/funding).

A taker round trip starts at roughly 1.8 bps in fees, plus the entry and exit half-spreads and slippage. Income depends on the size of favorable moves after matching, and on fills, losses, and turnover. A 55% direction hit rate alone says little about any of those. Hyperliquid publishes a co-located order-to-end-to-end median of 200 ms and p99 of 900 ms. These are reference points, with no claim that the simulated order-to-match delays mirror the venue's full latency distribution. The public API relays through nodes and has distinct fees for priority. [HyperCore overview](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/overview), [API servers](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/api-servers), [Priority fees](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/api/priority-fees).

## Candidate strategies

| Candidate | Economic premise | Test or missing evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local book pressure and midpoint reversion | A lopsided queue and depletion may forecast the next repricing. Momentary spread/loss of one side can move the mid by itself. | Local ridge baseline. Ask whether midpoint forecast skill survives a marketable limit and two spreads. |
| Intra-venue lead–lag | A liquid index or several peers may absorb common news first. A target that moves much more than both may mean-revert. | Lane Z cross-market ridge. Ablate index and peers against the local-only baseline. Use the index alone in the weekend/holiday cohort after measuring sparse peer coverage. |
| Same-stock cross-venue lag | A separate USDT perp reference may update first. Its basis against the USDC perp can drift due to different funding, collateral, liquidity and oracle inputs. | Test Binance NVDAUSDT or TSLAUSDT at actual capture times. Center the short-term basis on a trailing average. Trade the Hyperliquid leg only. Compare against the complete Hyperliquid-only model and add source-feed delay. |
| Passive quote skew | Capture spread while placing offers on the side that the short-horizon fair-value forecast favors. | Needs price-time queue reconstruction, trades, a cancel- and fill-latency model, inventory limits, and adverse-selection markouts. BBO size and price alone cannot establish the fills. |
| Cash or futures lead | A direct Nasdaq NVDA/TSLA or CME NQ quote may provide a different information source during cash hours. | Needs synchronized, licensed cash/futures depth with operator receipt time, a separately frozen test, and evidence that the lead survives routing. An oracle or candle series does not establish that timing. |
| Funding or reopen basis | A perp basis may converge as external prices resume, or persist long enough to earn net carry across venues. | Requires per-leg funding, settlement clocks, hedging costs, and liquidation/margin checks. This is often a longer holding-period trade even if routing operates in milliseconds. |

Binance listed its independently priced USDT-margined TSLA contract January 28 and NVDA March 26, 2026, covering the April–October regular study dates. Its contracts are inputs only, so Lane Z includes no simulated hedge or Binance execution P&L. [Binance NVDA listing](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/detail/1aa8f5f80e4a4afcab149e653fd3113d), [Binance TSLA listing](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/detail/40c76b4deaa247f09774e5d1ee747cb8).

## What prior work changes

- **Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov** find that order-flow imbalance at the best quotes explains short-interval price changes on the NYSE, with stronger impact when the book is shallow. Much of that relationship is contemporaneous. Lane Z measures past quote changes and evaluates only movement after entry arrival. **Gould and Bonart** find one-tick-ahead queue-imbalance skill especially for large-tick equities. Their target is a midpoint move, so taking both sides of the spread calls for a separate test. [Cont et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.6402), [Gould and Bonart](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03492).
- **Cont, Cucuringu and Zhang** report that cross-asset order flow can help predict future short-horizon returns, even where integrated own-asset order flow explains most contemporaneous price movement. **Kolm, Turiel and Westray** report short-lived forecasts, with stationary order-flow inputs helping compared with raw book states. These motivate local and cross-asset ridge before spending degrees of freedom on a deep model. [Cross-impact of order flow imbalance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.13213), [Deep order flow imbalance](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3900141).
- **Hasbrouck** uses exchange timestamps at high resolution and studies where information enters the U.S. equity market, including NVDA. **Budish, Cramton and Shim** document that index/futures price disparities can be short-lived and competition changes who captures them. These findings motivate receipt-time joins and delay stress, even if time stamps suggest a source leads. [Price Discovery in High Resolution](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~jh4/Research/HRVAR/HRVAR08.pdf), [The High-Frequency Trading Arms Race](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2388265).
- **Avellaneda and Stoikov** explicitly account for the inventory/market-making tradeoff. Hyperliquid's HLP combines several market-making strategies with liquidation and fee income. Public HLP returns cannot be attributed to a Lane Z signal, so passive quoting needs its own fill and inventory test. [Avellaneda and Stoikov](https://math.nyu.edu/inmemoriam/avellaneda/HighFrequencyTrading.pdf), [Hyperliquid protocol vaults](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/hypercore/vaults/protocol-vaults).
- **Aperiodic's public study** reports stronger predictability for Trade[XYZ] stock perps when underlying markets are closed. It uses 5-minute bars, a 15-minute target, and a larger universe, and identifies execution latency and funding as limitations. That result motivates a separate weekend/holiday arm. A seconds-scale result cannot confirm or refute the 15-minute finding. [Szemerey and Szulyovszky](https://blog.aperiodic.io/p/closed-market-stock-perps).
- **Coin Metrics** studies the distinct price discovery and premiums of equity perps versus tokenized stocks and underlying equities. Its NVDAx-to-cash lead/lag example supports investigating venue and instrument differences. NVDAx is a separate instrument from the perp in Lane Z. [Coin Metrics study](https://coinmetrics.substack.com/p/stock-market-that-never-sleeps-inside).

## Market-data reality

Tardis publishes free, original-time-order first-of-month samples and supplies both event and collection timestamps. Its `book_ticker` records the venue's native best-price feed. Hyperliquid capture infrastructure is documented as Tokyo. Independent exchange capture clocks and paths can differ. Lane Z preserves both clocks and tests an added Binance feed delay. The results include file URLs, digests, row counts, gaps, and observed ages. The quotes stay local and can be fetched from the provider. [Tardis CSV format](https://docs.tardis.dev/downloadable-csv-files), [Tardis Hyperliquid collection](https://docs.tardis.dev/historical-data-details/hyperliquid), [Tardis Binance Futures collection](https://docs.tardis.dev/historical-data-details/binance-futures).

The official exchange archive offers L2 snapshots and node output via requester-pays S3. A non-validator node can write order book diffs and snapshots with price-time ordering for queue research. TickFoundry documents a per-block reconstructed book and Bitquery documents order-level updates. Greater depth and more frequent quotes improve fill simulation, while local receipt timing remains a separate measurement. [Hyperliquid archive](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/historical-data), [Hyperliquid node formats](https://hyperliquid.gitbook.io/hyperliquid-docs/for-developers/nodes/l1-data-schemas), [TickFoundry](https://tickfoundry.com/hyperliquid-historical-data), [Bitquery data schema](https://docs.bitquery.io/docs/cloud/hyperliquid/).

## Follow-up on strategies described in public

- **Cross-venue taking.** Albers, Cucuringu, Howison, and Shestopaloff reported that linear models explained 10–37% of 500 ms Bitcoin return variation, depending on the target exchange. Their taker tests offered only marginal returns in a small subset of markets with steep fee discounts. Lane Z saw the same split between predicting a move and capturing it. In a November 21 Hyperliquid checkpoint, all four Binance-input orders exceeded the 2 bp price cushion at modeled arrival. An ex-post 25 bp cushion filled all four at 500 ms and lost $0.2343. [Albers et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.09750)
- **Passive quotes.** Lehalle and Mounjid show that forecasts of liquidity-consuming flow lose value when a market maker lacks time to cancel and replace orders. Hummingbot's cross-exchange strategy posts passively and sends the hedge after a fill. It checks profitability against the hedge venue before quoting. Both studies motivate modeling queue priority, cancellation time, inventory and executable hedge quotes. A Reddit practitioner reported weaker live P&L after replacing midpoint fills with actual spreads. The Reddit report is anecdotal. [Lehalle and Mounjid](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.00261) · [Hummingbot strategy](https://hummingbot.org/strategies/v1-strategies/cross-exchange-market-making/) · [Reddit execution discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/algotrading/comments/1tnl249/the_single_biggest_gap_between_my_backtests_and/) · [Lane Z order-level test](l4-smoke.md)
- **Quarter-hour dislocations.** Aperiodic reports 366 closed-hours trades across 27 equity perps at 11.00 bps per trade with 1.5 bps fees per side. The median hold was 15 minutes. Typical entries followed large price moves and unusually wide deviation from the contract's own oracle premium. The base run uses bar-end bid and ask without order latency or funding. A five-minute entry delay reduces the result to 3.58 bps per trade. With that delay and 3 bps fees per side, it falls to 0.58 bps with a day-clustered t-statistic of 0.18. The input code can carry forward quotes and the replay supplies no quote-age or available-depth test. Lane Z uses fresh executable quotes, modeled arrival times, and funding guards for its independent test. The pinned public version contains code and aggregate results. The original five-minute history requires a data key. [Aperiodic study](https://blog.aperiodic.io/p/closed-market-stock-perps) · [Published results](https://github.com/aperiodic-io/notebooks/blob/0a2bf36b531fc320f549ae3ef58f7aa5380094ae/research/closed-market-stock-perps/results.json) · [Input code](https://github.com/aperiodic-io/notebooks/blob/0a2bf36b531fc320f549ae3ef58f7aa5380094ae/research/closed-market-stock-perps/data.py#L86-L109) · [Replay code](https://github.com/aperiodic-io/notebooks/blob/0a2bf36b531fc320f549ae3ef58f7aa5380094ae/research/closed-market-stock-perps/model.py#L166-L211)
- **Weekend prices carry information.** Blockworks reported that Hyperliquid weekend moves reduced the median error in estimating the eventual reopening price on its market-weekend sample. It studied the information in late-weekend prices. Trade entry, exit and costs are a different empirical question, which Lane Z tests at explicitly modeled arrival times. [Blockworks weekend study](https://app.blockworks.com/report/weekend-trading-evidence-of-price-discovery-in-hyperliquid-s-weekend-markets)
